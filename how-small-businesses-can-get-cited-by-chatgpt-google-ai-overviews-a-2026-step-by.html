Introduction

As of October 16, 2026, generative AI is a primary way many people start research and discovery. Google’s AI Overviews (formerly SGE) and ChatGPT’s web-enabled answers now routinely summarize the web and include clickable source links — which means small businesses that appear in those summaries can get major visibility without traditional clickthroughs. This playbook gives a practical, prioritized step-by-step plan you can implement this quarter to improve the chances your business is referenced and cited by ChatGPT and Google AI Overviews. It’s grounded in the official guidance from Google and OpenAI and recent research into AI citation behavior. (developers.google.com)

Quick primer: how these AI systems pick sources (short)

  • Google’s generative features ground answers in pages from its Search index using retrieval-augmented generation (RAG) and related techniques; the same core ranking systems matter for AI Overviews as for classic Search. That means standard SEO and site quality are still foundational. (developers.google.com)
  • ChatGPT’s “Search” mode and OpenAI’s web-search API fetch and summarize web results and return inline URL citations when web tools are used — you can make your site eligible by ensuring it’s crawlable by OpenAI’s published searchbot addresses and by following general indexing best practices. (help.openai.com)
  • Both systems favor sources that are crawlable, authoritative, clearly structured, and that contain concise, verifiable facts (claims, dates, specific numbers) they can quote or paraphrase. Google’s product updates also emphasize surfacing direct links and article suggestions inside AI Overviews. (blog.google)

Now the playbook.

Step 1 — Make your site reliably discoverable (technical basics)

Actions (first 1–2 weeks) - Verify your site in Google Search Console and confirm no-index/robots blocks are preventing indexing. For AI Overviews, pages must first be indexable and eligible to show a snippet. (developers.google.com)
- Ensure fast mobile performance (Core Web Vitals), clear semantic HTML, and proper canonical tags. AI systems still depend on standard crawling and rendering, so treat this as table stakes. (developers.google.com)
- Allow public crawlers: confirm your CDN, firewall, or robots settings allow traffic from OpenAI’s published searchbot IPs if you want ChatGPT to be able to fetch your pages. (OpenAI documents this requirement.) (help.openai.com)

Why it matters: If a page isn’t crawlable or indexed, neither Google’s AI Overviews nor ChatGPT’s search-enabled answers can cite it.

Step 2 — Structure the facts so an AI can extract and cite them

Actions (weeks 2–4) - Add clear structured data where appropriate (LocalBusiness/Organization, Product, FAQPage, Article). Use schema.org types on service pages, contact pages, product pages, and key blog posts. Google doesn’t require schema to show in AI Overviews, but structured data helps machines parse facts faster. (developers.google.com)
- Add concise, scannable “facts at a glance” blocks: one-line location, hours, prices, service areas, certifications, and one-sentence answers to frequent buyer questions. Put those near the top in HTML (not only images). AI summarizers frequently pull short declarative facts. (developers.google.com)

Why it matters: AI answers favor pages where the fact to cite is explicit and unambiguous.

Step 3 — Publish non-commodity original content & data

Actions (weeks 3–8) - Create 1–3 lead assets that only you can produce: original case studies, before/after data, local market micro-studies, proprietary checklists, or small surveys. Originality and depth differentiate you from “commodity” content that many sites can replicate. (developers.google.com)
- Format these assets with clear headings, short summary paragraphs, and one-page PDFs or datasets (with metadata) so crawlers can index the content and cite it precisely.

Why it matters: Both Google’s guidance and observed behavior from AI systems show original, experience-based content is more likely to be surfaced and cited.

Step 4 — Create FAQ and answer snippets optimized for citation

Actions (ongoing) - Add an FAQ section with short Q→A pairs (1–2 sentences each), and include dates and specific numbers when relevant. Use FAQPage schema if it fits. Avoid keyword-stuffed, vague answers; aim for crisp, verifiable facts. (developers.google.com)
- Where applicable, add HowTo or step-lists for service processes (simple, numbered) even if you don’t want the rich result — clarity helps AI select the right excerpt.

Why it matters: AI systems tend to extract short, authoritative answers for summarization and links.

Step 5 — Signal authority off-site (citations, reviews, partnerships)

Actions (1–3 months) - Encourage consistent online citations: local directories, industry associations, partner sites, and customer reviews (Google Business Profile, Yelp). Positive, verified external references help ranking and provide the social proof AIs prefer. (developers.google.com)
- Publish one guest post or data mention on a higher-authority local or industry site and link back to your case study or dataset. AI summarizers frequently choose sources that are linked from other reputable pages.

Why it matters: Retrieval systems weight authority and corroboration; being referenced by other respected sources increases the chance you’ll be selected and cited.

Step 6 — Opt-in and visibility controls for platform-specific features

Actions (weeks 1–4) - Google: Check the “Generative AI performance” features inside Search Console and confirm inclusion for your site; follow Google’s policy and indexing steps. (developers.google.com)
- OpenAI: If you want ChatGPT to include your pages in Search-enabled responses, ensure OAI-Searchbot can crawl your domain and that you aren’t blocking the published OpenAI addresses. OpenAI’s docs describe how sites become eligible. (help.openai.com)

Why it matters: These platform-level controls affect eligibility and measurement.

Step 7 — Measure, iterate, and protect your conversions

Actions (ongoing) - Monitor Search Console’s generative AI reports and traditional Search traffic. Track impressions where your pages are shown in AI features and the downstream engagement (time on page, newsletter signups). (developers.google.com)
- Recognize that being cited can reduce click-throughs but increase brand visibility — the tradeoff many publishers have documented. Consider adding strong on-page CTAs (subscribe, book, call) and lead magnets so even a single AI-driven exposure converts. Recent studies show AI summaries can alter click behavior and publisher revenue dynamics, so plan for multiple conversion pathways (onsite lead gen, offline calls). (arxiv.org)

Track performance in Search Console and adapt: sometimes the win is not every click but the brand mention in a high-value summary.
