Apple Intelligence powers new on‑device and server‑assisted AI features across iPhone, iPad, and Mac — from smarter Siri conversations and message/email summaries to writing tools, image creation, and notification prioritization. If you prefer to limit those features for privacy, storage, battery, or personal reasons, Apple provides multiple controls: a master toggle, per‑feature switches, Screen Time restrictions, and opt‑outs for data‑sharing used to improve models. This guide shows exactly where to go and what each control does, with step‑by‑step instructions for iPhone/iPad and Mac plus tips for checking whether your requests go to Apple’s secure cloud (Private Cloud Compute).

Quick overview: the controls you’ll use

  • Master on/off: Settings → Apple Intelligence & Siri (iPhone/iPad) or System Settings → Apple Intelligence & Siri (Mac). (support.apple.com)
  • Per‑feature toggles: summary cards, message/mail summarization, writing tools, image tools, Call Context, etc., available in the same settings or organized by app. (support.apple.com)
  • Screen Time restrictions (strongest option for kids or to block features): Screen Time → Content & Privacy Restrictions → Intelligence & Siri. Use this to “Don’t Allow” Writing Tools, Image Creation, Extensions, Siri AI, etc. (support.apple.com)
  • Data‑sharing opt‑out (don’t let Apple use your interactions to train models): Settings → Privacy & Security → Analytics & Improvements → “Improve Siri & Apple Intelligence” (toggle off). (apple.com)
  • Transparency / audit: export the Apple Intelligence Report (Privacy & Security → Apple Intelligence Report) to see which requests were handled on‑device vs. sent to Apple’s Private Cloud Compute. (security.apple.com)

Below are clear step‑by‑step instructions and what to expect after each change.

How to turn Apple Intelligence off completely (iPhone & iPad)

  1. Open Settings.
  2. Tap Apple Intelligence & Siri.
  3. Toggle off Apple Intelligence (the master switch). Confirm if prompted.

What this does: disables the built‑in Apple Intelligence features and prevents the system from doing AI‑based summaries, writing tools, image playground features, and many “smart” suggestions. You can still use standard, non‑AI functionality in apps. Apple documents this master switch path for iPhone and iPad. (support.apple.com)

Note: on some versions or devices you may also need to switch off Siri AI separately or revert to “Siri Classic” in the same settings if a separate Siri AI toggle appears. (support.apple.com)

How to turn Apple Intelligence off on Mac

  1. Click the Apple menu → System Settings.
  2. Click Apple Intelligence & Siri in the sidebar.
  3. Toggle Apple Intelligence off (or click Turn Off Apple Intelligence if shown). Confirm if prompted.

You can also disable Siri AI specifically under Siri settings and turn off per‑app summarization and smart replies inside Mail, Messages, Notifications, and the Phone/Journal apps. Apple documents the Mac paths for each feature. (support.apple.com)

Disable only specific features (if you want partial control)

If you like some AI features but not others, disable features individually:

  • Message summaries: Settings → Apple Intelligence & Siri → Apps → Messages → Summarize Messages (off). (support.apple.com)
  • Mail summaries / smart replies: Settings → Apple Intelligence & Siri → Apps → Mail → Summarize Message Previews / Personalize Smart Replies (off). (support.apple.com)
  • Notification summaries / priority: Settings → Notifications → Summarize Notifications / Prioritize Notifications (off). (support.apple.com)
  • Writing Tools (keyboard proofread/rewrite): Settings → Screen Time → Content & Privacy Restrictions → Intelligence & Siri → Writing Tools → Don’t Allow (or turn off in the app-level settings). (support.apple.com)

Tip: Apple sometimes organizes controls by app (Mail, Messages, Phone). If you can’t find one setting in the Apple Intelligence pane, open the relevant app’s settings section. (support.apple.com)

Use Screen Time to block AI features (best for parents and managed devices)

  1. Open Settings → Screen Time.
  2. Turn on Content & Privacy Restrictions (if not already).
  3. Tap Content & Privacy Restrictions → Intelligence & Siri (or Siri).
  4. Set categories like “Writing Tools,” “Image Creation,” “Intelligence Extensions,” or “Siri AI” to Don’t Allow.

This method is useful if you want to enforce a restriction for a child’s device or lock down a device you manage — it prevents users from re‑enabling those features without the Screen Time passcode. Apple documents these Screen Time controls. (support.apple.com)

Stop Apple from using your Siri/AI interactions to improve models

Apple provides an explicit opt‑out for using your Siri/Dictation/Translate interactions to improve its systems and foundation models. To opt out: - Settings → Privacy & Security → Analytics & Improvements → turn off Improve Siri & Apple Intelligence (or Improve Siri & Dictation on older devices).

Apple’s legal/privacy pages explain that opting out prevents your eligible interactions from being retained for model improvement, though a small subset of interactions may be kept longer in some cases if you had previously opted in. If you want the clearest data‑sharing boundary, turn this toggle off. (apple.com)

See what ran locally vs. what went to the cloud (Apple Intelligence Report)

Apple gives transparency via the Apple Intelligence Report: - Settings → Privacy & Security → Apple Intelligence Report → Export Activity.
The exported JSON contains two main sections: modelRequests (all AI requests) and privateCloudComputeRequests (those handled by Apple’s Private Cloud Compute). This is the best way to verify whether particular kinds of requests (e.g., some writing tools) were processed on your device or sent to Apple’s cloud. (security.apple.com)

Why it matters: Apple runs many tasks on‑device, but for larger, more complex requests the system may use Apple’s Private Cloud Compute — a server‑side environment Apple designed to preserve privacy while providing extra compute. If you want zero‑cloud processing, disabling Apple Intelligence entirely is the simplest route; there is no per‑request “never send to cloud” user switch. (security.apple.com)

Practical tips, gotchas, and what to watch for

  • After turning off AI features you may free storage and reduce background activity — several users report reclaimed space when disabling local model files. Still, system data and cached items may take a restart or time to shrink. (macpaw.com)
  • Turning off Siri or Apple Intelligence can affect CarPlay voice features; some CarPlay behaviors expect Siri to be available. If CarPlay misbehaves after changes, check CarPlay settings and Siri toggles. (oemdtc.com)
  • If a device is managed (work or school) it’s possible admins can enforce AI settings via MDM or configuration profiles. If you can’t change a toggle, contact the device administrator. (support.apple.com)

Conclusion Apple gives multiple ways to control Apple Intelligence: a master off switch, per‑feature controls, parental Screen Time locks, and an explicit opt‑out for sharing interactions to improve models. If your priority is absolute avoidance of cloud‑assisted AI, turn off Apple Intelligence entirely and verify activity with the Apple Intelligence Report. If you prefer a mix (keep on‑device features but block cloud training or specific tools), toggle individual settings and opt out of analytics. For any critical or managed‑device questions, check with your administrator and review Apple’s support and privacy pages linked above. (support.apple.com)
