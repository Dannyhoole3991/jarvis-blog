Introduction Every local business can benefit from a friendly, always‑on assistant that answers customer questions, qualifies visitors, and collects contact details — without hiring another person. In 2026 the fastest ways to get that value are either: 1) a Custom GPT built inside ChatGPT (quick to create but limited in how it can be deployed), or 2) a no‑code embeddable chatbot platform trained on your site and files (fully embeddable, built for lead capture). This guide walks you through both zero‑code paths, gives practical scripts and qualification flows, and shows how to push leads into Google Sheets or your CRM using Zapier — no programming required. Important note: OpenAI has announced a phased retirement and migration of Custom GPTs, with creation and retirement dates you should check against your account before starting (see the OpenAI notice). (help.openai.com)

Quick decision: Which path is right for you?

  • If you only need an internal assistant or a quick prototype and you can still create a GPT in your workspace, build a Custom GPT in ChatGPT — it’s fast and no‑code. But these live on chatgpt.com and typically can only be shared as a link (visitors leave your website). (help.openai.com)
  • If you want a widget on your website that captures leads, books appointments, and integrates with your CRM without developers, use a no‑code chatbot platform (SiteGPT, InsiteChat, Tidio, etc.) that trains on your content and embeds with one script tag. This is zero‑code, embeddable, and built for lead capture. (insitechat.ai)

Below are two zero‑code step‑by‑step playbooks: one for a Custom GPT link and one for an embeddable lead‑capture bot. Choose the one that matches your deployment needs.

Playbook A — Build a Custom GPT (fast prototype / internal use)

  1. Prepare your content and goals
  2. Decide the single goal (e.g., “qualify roofing leads and collect name, phone, ZIP, timeline”).
  3. Gather up to 20 files you want the GPT to reference (pricing sheet, service area ZIP list, FAQ PDF). (help.openai.com)
  4. Open the GPT Builder
  5. Go to chatgpt.com → Explore GPTs → Create. Choose the conversational or configuration builder. Fill name, short description, and conversation starters. (help.openai.com)
  6. Write instructions (the AI’s rules)
  7. Include “tone,” “what to ask first,” and exact output formats. Example instruction snippet:
    • “Greet briefly. Ask 3 qualifying questions (service, ZIP code, timeline). If the customer provides phone or email, repeat back a confirmation and offer a Calendly link to book. If ZIP is outside our service area, politely explain and offer referral resources.”
  8. Put fixed rules (what to never do) into Instructions rather than knowledge files. (help.openai.com)
  9. Upload knowledge files
  10. Add your pricing, service area list, and FAQ so answers are grounded in your content. Test in Preview. (help.openai.com)
  11. Add conversation starters and examples
  12. Provide 3 real prompts visitors might use and sample ideal replies to tune behavior.
  13. Share the GPT
  14. Save and copy the GPT link. Remember: visitors will leave your site to use it; you won’t get built‑in analytics or automated CRM writes unless you add external actions (which often require additional setup or developer help). (help.openai.com)

When to pick this: quick internal playbooks, staff productivity tools, or proof‑of‑concepts. If you need an on‑site lead capture flow, use Playbook B.

Playbook B — Zero‑code embeddable lead bot (best for customer‑facing lead capture)

  1. Choose a no‑code chatbot provider
  2. Look for “trained on your website” + “embeddable script tag” + “Zapier/CRM integrations.” Popular examples: SiteGPT, InsiteChat, Chatbase, Tidio. They differ on price and features; all remove developer work. (insitechat.ai)
  3. Connect your content
  4. Point the tool at your website, upload PDFs and Google Drive docs (most platforms crawl your pages and build a knowledge base for grounded answers). (insitechat.ai)
  5. Design the qualification flow (keep it short)
  6. Best practice: start by answering the visitor’s question, then ask 3–5 qualification questions that change routing or follow‑up: service needed, ZIP/city, when (timeline), rough budget (range), contact info. Friendly, one question at a time. HubSpot and lead‑bot audits recommend minimizing friction and providing value first. (blog.hubspot.com)
  7. Example: “Hi — what can I help with today?” → classify intent → “Which ZIP is the job in?” → “When would you like this done?” → “Great — can I grab your name and phone so we can text you a quote?”
  8. Add appointment booking and CTAs
  9. Connect Calendly (or your scheduler) via the chatbot platform’s integrations so the bot can offer available slots and confirm bookings.
  10. Capture and route leads (Zapier, no code)
  11. Use the platform’s native Zapier integration or Webhooks to send captured leads to Google Sheets, HubSpot, or Slack. Zapier’s “Webhooks by Zapier” Catch Hook is a common, no‑code way to accept payloads and map fields to your destination. (help.zapier.com)
  12. Typical Zap: Trigger = Catch Hook; Action = Create Row in Google Sheets / Create Contact in HubSpot / Send Slack message to sales queue.
  13. Test and tune
  14. Simulate real visitor questions; make sure answers are grounded, qualification questions map to CRM fields, and handoffs to humans occur when required.
  15. Publish on your site
  16. Paste the single script tag into your site (WordPress, Shopify, Squarespace all support this). The bot runs in your domain and captures leads without requiring visitors to leave or log in. (insitechat.ai)

Measurement and operators’ playbook

  • Track: number of chats, qualified leads, booked appointments, conversion from chat → booked, and bot accuracy (edits or handoffs). HubSpot’s lead‑generation frameworks help you tie chat behavior to pipeline stages. (blog.hubspot.com)
  • Handoff rules: configure the bot to escalate when a visitor asks for a person, when the answer is uncertain, or when the lead’s estimated value passes a threshold. Keep humans in the loop for high‑value interactions. (risalsystems.com)
